# Studies

**Small pieces of making used to understand a design decision with the hands.**

Studies are neither portfolio work nor mandatory exercises. They exist when recreating, varying, measuring, or implementing something will teach more than another paragraph.

A study can be tiny:

- rebuild one sidebar row;
- reproduce one title hierarchy;
- redraw an icon family at several optical weights;
- copy one transition and change only duration/easing;
- recreate a dense list and alter grouping intervals;
- sketch three ways to represent one state transition;
- implement a physical/product interaction metaphor in software;
- compare one mobile gesture against one visible control.

## Default loop

```text
reference / question
-> imitate closely enough to understand it
-> identify the important variables
-> alter one or two
-> use / compare
-> write what changed
```

Do not start with “make something cool.” Start with a question.

Useful questions include:

- Why does this hierarchy read immediately?
- How much spacing actually creates the grouping?
- Which part of this animation communicates causality?
- Why does this dense interface stay calm?
- What happens when the labels disappear?
- Which visual details carry identity at tiny scale?
- Why does this control still feel native after being customized?

## Suggested study note

Keep it short unless the work earns more detail.

```md
# <study>

Question:
Reference:
What I copied:
What I changed:
What became obvious only after making it:
What I would carry into real work:
What remains uncertain:
```

Screenshots, source files, measurements, and little runnable prototypes can live beside the note.

## Relationship to the rest of Tact

A study can begin from [`RESERVOIR.md`](../RESERVOIR.md) and later inform:

- an applied [`case-study`](../case-studies/);
- an earned [`pattern`](../patterns/);
- a preference in [`SIGNATURE.md`](../SIGNATURE.md).

Most studies do not need promotion. Their job is to sharpen the eye and hand.

## Anti-homework rule

Do not turn Tact into an art-school syllabus with 200 exercises waiting to be completed.

Prefer studies that answer a question from current work. Fundamental practice is welcome, especially typography, hierarchy, layout, iconography, color, material, motion, accessibility, and interaction state, but it should stay connected to something Leo actually wants to understand or make.
