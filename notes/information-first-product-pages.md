# Information-first product pages

A study of product presentation for people who appreciate beauty and want the facts immediately.

The starting irritation is Leo's car-site complaint:

> The user already knows the wheels spin.

Too many product pages spend their opening screens proving that the object is desirable, premium, dramatic, alive, revolutionary, or photographed at sunset. The buyer arrives with harder questions:

```text
What is it?
What does it cost?
Will it fit?
Will it do the job?
Which version should I choose?
What compromises am I buying?
Where can I verify the claims?
```

A beautiful product page can answer those questions in the first screen and still feel gorgeous. Precision can be seductive. A dimension drawing can be beautiful. A clean comparison table can feel luxurious. A cutaway can create more desire than another cinematic pan because it reveals the object.

The strongest pattern across cars, cameras, computers, machine tools, architecture, hardware, and software is simple:

> **Put decision-useful truth in the path of desire. Let the product earn the story.**

This note studies examples, proposes an ideal page, and turns the judgment into experiments.

## 1. Information-first means decision-first

Information-first presentation is often confused with a giant specification dump. A giant undifferentiated table creates its own fog.

The useful unit is the **decision**.

A buyer evaluating a car wants range, dimensions, cargo, weight, power, charging, towing, price, availability, and variant differences. A camera buyer wants sensor, lens mount, dimensions, weight, storage, stabilization, dynamic range, codecs or file formats, battery, and operating limits. A laptop buyer wants dimensions, weight, display, ports, battery, processor, memory, storage, repair path, OS support, and price.

The opening screen should answer the few questions that eliminate or qualify the product.

That gives a useful hierarchy:

```text
identity
-> decisive facts
-> fit and constraints
-> comparison
-> engineering explanation
-> full reference
```

The page can remain visually rich throughout. The visual hierarchy follows the buyer's uncertainty.

### Claim

**Lead with the facts that can change the decision.**

A dramatic photograph can share the first screen with price, availability, dimensions, and key performance data. The conflict comes from page order, space allocation, and interaction cost.

### Experiment: the five-question retrieval test

Build two versions of the same product page:

1. cinematic opening: full-screen photography, motion, headline, feature story, specifications later;
2. information-first opening: canonical image, price, availability, six to eight decisive facts, variant selector, compare and full-spec links.

Give participants five questions such as:

- What does it cost?
- Will it fit within a known width or rack height?
- Which variant meets a performance threshold?
- Which port, mount, battery, or accessory standard does it use?
- What is one meaningful tradeoff?

Measure:

- time to answer;
- scroll distance;
- wrong answers;
- backtracks;
- confidence;
- number of page regions visited.

The cinematic version has to earn its extra attention with measurable gains in understanding, confidence, or desire.

## 2. Cars: the clearest battlefield

Car sites make the conflict vivid because the products are visually seductive and technically constrained.

### Porsche 911 GT3: beauty with useful performance near the front

The current [Porsche 911 GT3 page](https://www.porsche.com/canada/en/models/911/911-gt3-models/911-gt3/) puts the car in glamorous presentation while surfacing major performance facts close to the top: acceleration, power, and later top speed, torque, engine type, variant comparison, and links into technical data.

The strong part is the coexistence. The car still gets the hero treatment. Performance values remain visible enough to anchor the desire in something real.

Porsche's older technical fact-sheet style goes further: side elevation, dimensions, wheelbase, mass, engine data, and performance can coexist in one compact graphic. That graphic has the density of a catalogue page and the romance of a technical drawing.

Steal:

- canonical side/profile imagery;
- a compact performance strip;
- explicit variant comparison;
- dimension drawings as visual objects.

Push further:

- price and availability beside the identity;
- dimensions, cargo, fuel or charging facts earlier;
- “what changes between variants?” as a first-class view.

### Mercedes EQS: one brand demonstrates both modes

The [Mercedes-Benz EQS SUV class page](https://www.mercedes-benz.ca/en/vehicles/class/eqs/suv) opens in category-story mode: “The SUV, reinvented. The EV, reimagined,” followed by chapters for introduction, innovation, design and luxury, performance and charging, and gallery.

For an information-first buyer, this opening spends too much time establishing that an expensive electric SUV can be luxurious.

The [specific Mercedes-Maybach EQS 680 SUV model page](https://www.mercedes-benz.ca/en/vehicles/model/eqs/suv/eqs680z4) is dramatically better for decision work. It surfaces towing, torque, power, exterior dimensions, curb weight, interior dimensions, cargo capacity, battery capacity, charging rates and times, range, acceleration, and turning circle.

Same company, same object family, radically different usefulness.

Lesson:

> **Category pages often perform aspiration. Model pages often contain the truth. Bring model-page truth forward.**

### Tesla Model 3 owner manual: diagrams answer spatial questions cleanly

The [Model 3 dimensions page](https://www.tesla.com/ownersmanual/model3/en_eu/GUID-56562137-FC31-4110-A13C-9A9FC6657BF0.html) uses an annotated exterior drawing with labeled dimensions.

That kind of drawing answers “will it fit?” more directly than another three-quarter photograph. It also creates credibility because the page commits to measurable geometry.

### Car-page failure pattern

The recurring weak sequence looks like this:

```text
full-screen car
-> full-screen car moving
-> full-screen wheel detail
-> slogan
-> full-screen cabin
-> another slogan
-> animation
-> one performance number
-> configurator
-> specifications somewhere else
```

The buyer already accepts that the car has wheels, paint, seats, headlights, and motion.

The useful sequence is closer to:

```text
car + identity + price + availability
-> decisive specs
-> dimensions / cargo / charging / fuel / towing
-> variants and tradeoffs
-> engineering
-> useful gallery
-> ownership
-> full technical reference
```

The page can still be cinematic. Cinema gets a smaller, more deliberate role.

## 3. Cameras: romance earns its place when it reveals the instrument

Camera brands live close to art, so storytelling can carry genuine value. The test is whether the story increases understanding of the instrument.

### Leica M11: beautiful brochure, excellent reference page

The [Leica M11 product page](https://leica-camera.com/en-US/photography/cameras/m/m11-black) opens with romantic language about the M and the act of photography. Technical facts arrive inside that narrative: resolution options, dynamic range, ISO range, shutter speed, display, internal storage, and battery.

Leica also publishes a dedicated [M11 technical specification page](https://leica-camera.com/en-US/photography/cameras/m/m11-black/technical-specification) with exact dimensions, weight, materials, sensor details, mount, storage, interfaces, and operating conditions.

This two-page split works as a reference system, yet it imposes a mode switch. The ideal information-first page can preserve Leica's atmosphere while keeping a compact technical rail permanently within reach.

### Hasselblad X2D 100C: engineering numbers can carry the story

The [Hasselblad X2D 100C page](https://www.hasselblad.com/x-system/x2d-100c/) uses gorgeous photography and product imagery, then ties major sections to concrete engineering data: 100 MP, 16-bit color, 15 stops of dynamic range, phase-detection autofocus coverage, multi-axis stabilization, and internal SSD storage.

The separate [store specification view](https://store-na.hasselblad.com/products/x2d-100c) goes deeper into sensor dimensions, construction, formats, ISO, storage, body dimensions, and weight.

This is a strong pattern:

```text
claim
-> mechanism
-> number
-> image that demonstrates the consequence
```

The story becomes legible because the instrument remains present.

### Camera-page lesson

A camera page should make the physical and optical system visible:

- front, back, top, and side views;
- sensor size;
- lens mount;
- controls and ports;
- dimensions and weight;
- media and storage;
- battery;
- stabilization and autofocus behavior;
- file formats and codecs;
- environmental limits;
- a sample gallery with exposure and lens metadata.

A sample image without capture metadata is decoration. A sample image with lens, aperture, shutter, ISO, crop state, and file availability becomes evidence.

## 4. Computers and modular hardware: reference depth creates desire

Computers expose one of the strongest opportunities for information-first beauty because buyers routinely compare exact capabilities.

### Framework Laptop 13 and 13 Pro: very close to the target

The [Framework Laptop 13 specification view](https://frame.work/ca/en/laptop13?slug=laptop-diy-13-gen-amd&tab=specs) gives the page explicit modes such as Overview, Upgrades, Specs, Linux, and Downloads and guides.

Its specification content covers dimensions, weight, display options, expansion-card slots and port choices, battery, camera, processor options, sustained and boost power modes, thermal details, storage, memory, repairability, warranty, and Linux support. It even treats assembly time and difficulty as product facts.

The [Framework Laptop 13 Pro](https://frame.work/ca/en/laptop13pro?tab=specs) pushes this further with repairability and upgradeability in the product identity, then backs the claims with dimensions, power modes, thermals, ports, battery data, and component standards.

This earns credibility because the company exposes the machine as a machine.

Steal:

- stable tabs for overview/specs/upgrades/Linux/downloads;
- exact standards and interfaces;
- explicit assembly and service information;
- performance limits and operating modes;
- repair and upgrade paths treated as product features.

### Apple MacBook Pro: world-class reference, separated from brochure mode

The [MacBook Pro product page](https://www.apple.com/macbook-pro/) is a polished visual performance. It leads with highlights, imagery, video, and feature chapters.

The [MacBook Pro technical specifications page](https://www.apple.com/ca/macbook-pro/specs/) is exceptionally complete: chip configurations, memory, display size and resolution, brightness, refresh behavior, battery, ports, external-display support, dimensions, weight, and operating conditions.

Apple demonstrates that a company can produce both spectacular product theater and excellent technical reference. The information-first opportunity is to reduce the distance between them.

A sticky “Key specs / Compare / Full specs” rail on the main page could preserve the spectacle while giving impatient buyers immediate access to the reference layer.

### Raspberry Pi: boring facts become trust

The [Raspberry Pi 5 product brief](https://datasheets.raspberrypi.com/rpi5/raspberry-pi-5-product-brief.pdf) includes processor, GPU, memory options, display support, USB, Ethernet, PCIe, power, production lifetime, list pricing, and even reliability information.

The [Compute Module 5 page](https://www.raspberrypi.com/products/compute-module-5/) leads with dimensions and a compact list of SoC, RAM, eMMC, radio, Ethernet, PCIe, USB, GPIO, HDMI, and MIPI capabilities.

This is excellent technical-product behavior. Long production commitment, operating requirements, connector standards, and reliability figures may look visually plain, yet they carry enormous purchasing value.

> **Boring facts are often the most persuasive facts.**

## 5. Industrial tools: the job arrives before the brand story

Industrial products tend to present information better because vague copy fails quickly when a buyer needs to drill a hole, cut a part, fit a machine, meet a tolerance, or calculate throughput.

### Hilti TE 6-22: sell the tool into the task

The [Hilti TE 6-22 rotary hammer page](https://www.hilti.ca/c/CLS_POWER_TOOLS_7125/CLS_ROTARY_HAMMERS_7125/r13250305) pairs the product identity with weight, optimal hammer-drilling range, impact energy, kit configuration, price, technical data, features, and applications.

This says: here is the tool, here is the work it suits, here are the limits, here is the cost.

Steal the job-centered order.

### Haas VF-2: the product page behaves like a machine catalogue

The [Haas VF-2 page](https://www.haascnc.com/machines/vertical-mills/vf-series/models/small/vf-2.html) opens with the machine's working envelope and key properties: travels, taper, axes, spindle speed, tool capacity. Dense tables follow for travels, spindle, torque, table, feed rates, and other data. Comparison and brochure access sit nearby.

This page respects a buyer who may already know the category and wants to determine fit.

### Blackmagic URSA Cine: commerce and technical reference can coexist

The [Blackmagic URSA Cine technical specifications](https://www.blackmagicdesign.com/ca/products/blackmagicursacine/techspecs/W-URSA-61) put product identity, price, technical description, sensor dimensions, pixel pitch, lens mount, recording details, manuals, and software in one reference-heavy view.

The page feels closer to a manual than an ad, which suits an expensive professional instrument.

### Prusa CORE One: progressive disclosure done well

The [Prusa CORE One](https://www.prusa3d.com/product/prusa-core-one/) publishes build volume, filament diameter, layer height, dimensions, weight, mainboard, extruder, temperatures, connectivity, sensors, and supported materials. It also provides direct product comparison.

The [CORE One L](https://www.prusa3d.com/product/prusa-core-one-l/) offers a compact top specification set with an explicit “Show full specifications” control.

That is progressive disclosure with a clear contract:

```text
important facts now
-> full facts on request
```

The control compresses reference depth without hiding the existence of that depth.

## 6. Architecture: drawings, credits, and dimensions make photography more powerful

Architecture presentation proves that information density can increase visual pleasure.

### ArchDaily project pages: metadata before prose

Projects such as [Winona House](https://www.archdaily.com/600228/winona-house-25-8-research-design) lead with useful project metadata: architects, location, area, year, photography, and credits. Projects such as [Small House](https://www.archdaily.com/933701/small-house-alejandro-soffia) combine photography with plans and sections.

The page lets the reader admire the building while knowing what it is, where it is, how large it is, who made it, and how the spaces relate.

The [Tammisto Electricity Substation](https://www.archdaily.com/1018531/tammisto-electricity-substation-virkkunen-and-co-architects) is especially instructive because the project requires engineering explanation. Sections and imagery sit beside discussion of concrete framing, facade systems, equipment requirements, natural light, materials, modeling, and safety.

The beauty comes partly from understanding how the building works.

### BIG: portfolio browsing and project evaluation are different tasks

The [BIG homepage](https://big.dk/) is a huge, image-led project field. It works well for discovery, authorship, and browsing a body of work.

For a reader evaluating one building, a portfolio catalogue gives too little immediate technical context. Area, year, team, client, plans, sections, material systems, environmental strategy, and engineering credits belong in the first project view.

Lesson:

> **A gallery is a browsing interface. A project page is an evaluation interface.**

Mixing those jobs can make a beautiful site feel evasive.

## 7. Software landing pages: show the product doing the work

Software has fewer physical dimensions, which makes vague storytelling especially tempting. The useful substitute for a blueprint is visible behavior: interface state, input, output, limits, integrations, pricing, and system boundaries.

### Linear: product behavior becomes the visual language

The [Linear homepage](https://linear.app/) defines the product quickly, then shows actual issue, backlog, planning, intake, monitoring, automation, and shipping workflows.

The screenshots and live-looking interface fragments carry information. They demonstrate objects, fields, hierarchy, and flow. Visual drama comes from the product itself.

A stronger information-first version would bring pricing, plan limits, supported integrations, permissions, import/export behavior, and other purchasing constraints closer to the opening.

### PostHog: pricing and product facts can live on the homepage

The [PostHog homepage](https://posthog.com/) gives concrete free tiers and usage pricing for products such as analytics, replay, feature flags, and data tools, while keeping docs and developer access close.

This reduces the need for a buyer to open five tabs just to answer “what does this include?” and “what happens after the free tier?”

The page also gains credibility from a tone that acknowledges developer suspicion of sales ceremony.

### Raycast: a precise first sentence plus visible capabilities

The [Raycast homepage](https://www.raycast.com/) says what the product is immediately: an extendable launcher and collection of productivity tools. It follows with keyboard-first interaction, extensions, automation, reliability claims, and AI capabilities.

Specificity makes the visual polish feel earned.

### Superhuman and Notion: high-level promises dominate the opening

The [Superhuman homepage](https://superhuman.com/) leads with a time-saving promise and a claim of email productivity, then reveals features across subsequent sections.

The [Notion product page](https://www.notion.com/product) opens with broad promises around teams, agents, context, answers, and automation before the buyer reaches exact plan, limit, integration, import/export, or administrative details.

These pages work as brand narratives. For an information-first buyer, the opening needs a compact decision panel:

- price or starting price;
- supported platforms;
- core object model;
- major integrations;
- data import/export;
- offline behavior;
- collaboration and permission model;
- AI inclusion and usage limits;
- security/compliance links;
- comparison against adjacent plans.

Software has specs. Product pages should act like it.

## 8. Specs versus storytelling

The useful question is where storytelling earns screen space.

Story earns its place when it does one of four jobs:

1. explains why an engineering decision exists;
2. demonstrates a consequence the specification alone fails to communicate;
3. helps the buyer understand a tradeoff;
4. reveals authorship, intention, or context that changes appreciation of the object.

Weak story says:

> Engineered for uncompromising performance.

Useful story says:

> We chose a naturally aspirated 4.0 L engine for throttle response and high-rpm character; the choice gives up some low-rpm torque and efficiency compared with a turbocharged alternative.

Weak story says:

> Advanced thermal management.

Useful story says:

> The cooling system sustains this processor at X W under Y ambient condition; the higher mode increases fan noise by Z dBA.

Weak story says:

> Crafted for creators.

Useful story says:

> The body is machined aluminum because the mount registration and heat path need these tolerances; the result is X g of mass and Y operating range.

### Claim

**Story should explain a fact, consequence, or compromise.**

A designer's voice can be gorgeous when it points to a real decision.

### Experiment: engineering explanation formats

Present one feature in three forms:

1. adjective copy;
2. long narrative prose;
3. `decision -> mechanism -> measured result -> tradeoff`.

Ask participants to explain:

- what changed;
- how it works;
- what benefit it creates;
- what it costs.

Measure recall, trust, time, and willingness to rely on the claim without external research.

## 9. Visual drama versus decision-useful information

Visual drama has several legitimate jobs:

- establish scale;
- communicate material and finish;
- show use context;
- create desire;
- reveal movement;
- explain a mechanism;
- show before/after or state change;
- preserve spatial continuity through an interaction.

It earns less space when it repeats the same proposition.

A car driving through four landscapes still proves “car can move.” A laptop floating apart into layers can become useful when the animation labels replaceable components and stops at meaningful service steps. A camera shutter animation can be useful when it explains readout or rolling shutter. A CNC spindle animation can be useful when it shows axes, travel, clearance, or tool change.

### Motion budget

Treat motion like expensive editorial space.

For every animation, ask:

```text
What fact becomes easier to understand through time?
What relationship becomes visible only through movement?
Can the user skip it?
Does it delay access to information?
Does it still help on the tenth visit?
```

If the answer is mainly “it looks premium,” keep the motion brief and away from the path to facts.

### Experiment: motion budget test

Compare:

- autoplay cinematic sequences;
- user-triggered explanatory animation;
- still image + diagram.

Test first visit and tenth visit.

Measure:

- time to first useful fact;
- skip attempts;
- scroll interruption;
- comprehension of the mechanism;
- perceived beauty;
- perceived polish;
- return-visit irritation.

Motion that explains causality should survive. Repeated ceremony should collapse.

## 10. Progressive disclosure: compress depth, preserve access

A technical product can have hundreds of specifications. The page needs layers.

A useful four-level system:

### Level 0 — decision strip

Visible immediately:

- identity;
- one-sentence functional description;
- price;
- availability or lead time;
- six to eight decisive specs;
- current variant;
- compare;
- configure/buy;
- full specs.

### Level 1 — fit and constraints

One short scroll away:

- dimensions;
- weight;
- ports, mounts, interfaces, clearance;
- operating limits;
- compatibility;
- performance envelope;
- variant differences;
- common use cases with measurable thresholds.

### Level 2 — engineering and authorship

For people who want the why:

- annotated diagrams;
- cutaways;
- material choices;
- mechanism explanations;
- design commentary;
- measured results;
- compromises;
- development story attached to concrete decisions.

### Level 3 — full reference

Deep, searchable, linkable:

- complete specification ledger;
- manuals;
- datasheets;
- CAD/BIM;
- SDK/API docs;
- certifications;
- test methods;
- repair/service documentation;
- spare parts;
- firmware/software versions;
- change log.

Each level should have a stable anchor and deep link. A browser search should find values in the full reference. A buyer should be able to jump directly to dimensions or compatibility.

### Claim

**Progressive disclosure should reduce visual competition while preserving evidence.**

The deeper layer exists from the beginning. The interface simply gives each detail an appropriate visual weight.

### Experiment: three depths

Compare:

1. full table open by default;
2. six-to-eight-key-spec summary with full table one action away;
3. prose-only overview with specifications on a separate page.

Test quick qualification tasks and deep research tasks.

Measure task time, missed incompatibilities, perceived overload, use of search, and cross-page switching.

Prediction: #2 should win across the widest range because it keeps decisive facts immediate and reference depth close.

## 11. Comparison should expose tradeoffs

Marketing cards are weak comparison tools when each variant uses different adjectives, images, and feature order.

A serious comparison view normalizes the rows.

Example:

| | Base | Pro | Touring |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Price | $ | $$ | $$$ |
| Weight | value | value | value |
| Power / compute / capacity | value | value | value |
| Size | value | value | value |
| Battery / range / duty cycle | value | value | value |
| Key interface | value | value | value |
| Best fit | sentence | sentence | sentence |
| Main compromise | sentence | sentence | sentence |

Useful controls:

- **Differences only**
- **Show all**
- **Highlight best value per row**
- **Pin my current model**
- **Add competitor**
- **Change units**
- **Share this comparison**

The page should explain consequence alongside difference:

```text
+ 2 kg
-> larger battery
-> 3 additional hours under stated test
-> harder to carry
```

### Claim

**A comparison table should make the compromise legible.**

The user rarely needs a winner. The user needs the right compromise.

### Experiment: comparison formats

Give participants a constrained choice such as “choose the model under 1.5 kg with two external displays and the longest battery life.”

Compare:

- marketing cards;
- normalized matrix;
- differences-only matrix.

Measure selection accuracy, time, confidence, and number of details reopened.

## 12. Diagrams and blueprints: beauty that answers questions

Photography shows appearance, scale cues, materials, context, and emotional character.

Technical drawing shows relation.

Use drawings for:

- dimensions;
- wheelbase and overhang;
- port locations;
- rack units;
- service clearance;
- airflow;
- mounting points;
- sensor size;
- lens flange;
- hinge arc;
- cable exits;
- machine travel;
- tool envelope;
- room plan;
- section;
- load path;
- internal component map;
- exploded assembly;
- replacement sequence.

A strong drawing includes:

- units;
- labeled reference points;
- consistent orientation;
- a visible scale cue;
- variant differences where relevant;
- download in PDF/SVG/CAD when the audience can use it.

A blueprint can become the visual centerpiece of a page. Fine linework, restrained typography, and exact measurements carry their own elegance.

### Experiment: photo versus diagram

Give participants a fit problem: garage, doorway, desk, rack, camera cage, machine bay, lens clearance, room furniture, or cable routing.

Compare:

- photographs;
- dimension drawing;
- photographs + drawing.

Measure correct fit decisions, time, and confidence.

The combined version should win because each medium answers a different question.

## 13. Designer commentary: annotation beats manifesto

Designer commentary is valuable when it reveals intention attached to an observable detail.

Good format:

```text
[photo or drawing of detail]

“We moved this control 12 mm inward so it could be reached
without changing grip. That forced the card door to move,
which increased body depth by 1.5 mm.”

— designer
```

The comment contains a decision, constraint, and compromise.

A broad manifesto about purity, innovation, timelessness, or human-centered design gives the buyer little to inspect.

Use commentary as footnotes pinned to the object:

- Why this radius?
- Why this material?
- Why this hinge?
- Why this port placement?
- Why this room sequence?
- Why this cooling path?
- Why this control order?
- Why this algorithmic default?

This creates authorship without interrupting the reference layer.

### Experiment: commentary placement

Compare:

1. one full-screen designer manifesto;
2. short commentary attached to specific images and diagrams;
3. diagrams without commentary.

Ask participants to explain the design rationale and compromise 24 hours later.

Measure recall, perceived authorship, and time spent.

## 14. Photography should demonstrate

A technical product page benefits from a disciplined shot list.

### Canonical views

- front;
- back;
- left/right side;
- top;
- underside where relevant.

### Scale and fit

- object beside familiar reference;
- installed in a real environment;
- door/hinge/accessory open;
- cables attached;
- service access visible.

### Material and craft

- macro detail;
- seam, screw, knurling, texture, finish;
- internal component;
- edge treatment;
- mechanism.

### Use

- one real task;
- one difficult task;
- one hand/body interaction;
- one maintenance or upgrade interaction.

### Variants

- lineup from the same angle;
- identical scale;
- identical lighting;
- explicit labels.

### Evidence photography

For cameras, include capture metadata. For displays, explain source content and rendering conditions. For tools, show workpiece and operation. For architecture, pair hero photography with plans and sections. For repairable hardware, show the open device and replacement path.

A gallery becomes decision-useful when captions say what the image proves.

## 15. Credibility: publish the boring edges

Technical buyers often leave a product page to verify claims elsewhere. A credible page reduces that external scavenger hunt.

Strong credibility signals include:

- exact dimensions and weight;
- price and availability;
- test method and test conditions;
- tolerances;
- environmental limits;
- warranty;
- service interval;
- repair documentation;
- spare parts;
- production lifetime;
- certifications;
- manuals and datasheets;
- CAD/BIM files;
- SDK/API docs;
- supported standards;
- firmware/software requirements;
- compatibility lists;
- security documentation;
- energy use;
- acoustic data;
- benchmark methodology;
- version date;
- change log;
- named designers, engineers, consultants, and photographers.

Architecture pages gain credibility from credits because real work has many authors. Industrial products gain credibility from duty cycles and ranges. Raspberry Pi gains credibility from publishing production-lifetime commitments. Framework gains credibility from service and upgrade detail. Software gains credibility from pricing units, limits, migration paths, docs, status history, and export guarantees.

The most luxurious possible phrase on a technical product page may be:

> **Download datasheet.**

It says the seller expects inspection.

### Claim

**Credibility rises when the page exposes claims that can be checked.**

### Experiment: credibility ledger

Create two pages for the same fictional technical product.

Version A:
- awards;
- testimonials;
- brand claims;
- lifestyle imagery;
- generic feature headlines.

Version B:
- exact specs;
- test conditions;
- manuals;
- drawings;
- warranty;
- service parts;
- compatibility;
- team credits;
- downloadable datasheet.

Keep typography and photography equally polished.

Measure:

- trust;
- willingness to buy;
- willingness to deploy professionally;
- number of external verification searches;
- ability to explain limitations.

## 16. The ideal page: a museum catalogue crossed with a datasheet

The target is a page that feels like a beautiful service manual that happens to sell the product.

It should reward two tempos:

```text
10 seconds -> qualify the product
2 minutes  -> compare and understand it
20 minutes -> inspect engineering, authorship, and evidence
```

### First screen: the one-screen test

Before the second screenful, the user should be able to answer:

- What is this?
- What does it cost?
- Can I get it?
- Will it fit?
- Does it meet my main performance threshold?
- Which variant am I viewing?
- Where are comparison and complete specs?

Suggested desktop composition:

```text
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ PRODUCT                                  PRICE / AVAILABLE │
│ One sentence describing the actual job                    │
│                                                            │
│ ┌──────────────────────────┐  KEY SPECS                    │
│ │                          │  1. dimension / capacity      │
│ │ canonical product image  │  2. weight                   │
│ │                          │  3. performance               │
│ │                          │  4. interface / compatibility │
│ └──────────────────────────┘  5. power / range / battery   │
│                               6. major limit               │
│                                                            │
│ Variant [▼]   Compare   Full specs   Configure / Buy       │
│                                                            │
│ small dimension silhouette / critical fit diagram          │
└────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

The mobile version preserves the same order: identity, price, decisive facts, image, fit diagram, actions.

### Sticky reference rail

Keep a compact anchor bar available:

```text
Overview | Compare | Dimensions | Engineering | Gallery | Ownership | Specs | Docs
```

Include current variant, price, and compare state when space allows.

### Section 2: can it do my job?

Translate specifications into bounded applications.

Examples:

- drills masonry from X to Y diameter;
- sustains X W under Y test;
- fits a rack depth of X;
- carries Y liters behind row two;
- charges from A to B in C minutes at D kW;
- records format X at resolution/frame rate Y;
- supports monitor arrangement Z;
- prints material X at temperature Y;
- handles room span/load/occupancy constraints.

Keep the exact value beside the use statement.

### Section 3: dimensions and interfaces

Make the drawing large.

Show:

- front/side/top where needed;
- critical dimensions;
- ports and mounts;
- clearance;
- service access;
- cable exits;
- variant geometry.

Offer a PDF/SVG/CAD download when useful.

### Section 4: compare variants

Use a normalized matrix. Default to meaningful differences. Keep shared properties available.

Every large difference should have a consequence sentence.

### Section 5: engineering

Use modules built around:

```text
decision
-> mechanism
-> measurement
-> compromise
```

Pair each module with a cutaway, section, exploded view, thermal map, optical diagram, circuit block, floor plan, or real interface state.

### Section 6: gallery

Use fewer photographs with stronger jobs.

Every image gets a caption that says what the viewer can learn from it.

### Section 7: complete specifications

Make the full ledger:

- searchable;
- filterable;
- copyable;
- deep-linkable;
- available in metric/imperial where relevant;
- downloadable;
- versioned.

Use clear grouping and a visible “copy link to this row” affordance for expensive or technical products.

### Section 8: ownership and service

Include the facts that become important after purchase:

- warranty;
- maintenance;
- consumables;
- parts;
- repair;
- upgrade path;
- software support period;
- production lifetime;
- energy;
- noise;
- cleaning;
- storage;
- environmental limits;
- resale or transfer constraints where relevant.

### Section 9: documents and evidence

Put the serious files in one place:

- datasheet;
- manual;
- service manual;
- drawings;
- CAD/BIM;
- SDK/API;
- certificates;
- test reports;
- release notes;
- compatibility matrix;
- spare-parts list.

### Section 10: authorship and provenance

Credit:

- design;
- engineering;
- photography;
- architecture;
- consultants;
- manufacturing partners where meaningful.

Add short designer commentary tied to specific decisions.

This section can carry warmth and romance because the object has already earned the reader's trust.

## 17. Visual language for an information-first page

Information-first can be visually luxurious.

A fitting visual language could combine:

- a precise editorial grid;
- generous margins around dense facts;
- humanist sans serif for narrative;
- monospaced numerals for measurements and part numbers;
- thin rules and aligned units;
- large canonical photography;
- technical line drawings;
- restrained accent color for active comparison and callouts;
- motion used to explain mechanisms;
- persistent anchors;
- real materials and real environments.

The page should feel closer to a great industrial catalogue, camera manual, architectural monograph, or museum catalogue than a sequence of ad spots.

Beauty comes from proportion, typography, imagery, material detail, exactness, and restraint.

## 18. A scorecard for product pages

Score each category from 0–3.

| Category | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Identity | vague | category only | clear product | clear product + exact job |
| Price | absent | separate journey | available later | visible immediately |
| Key specs | absent | buried | compact summary | summary + context |
| Dimensions / fit | absent | prose | table | diagram + table |
| Comparison | absent | cards | common rows | normalized differences + consequences |
| Engineering | adjectives | story | mechanism | mechanism + measurement + compromise |
| Photography | decorative | attractive | useful views | useful views + evidence captions |
| Motion | blocking | decorative | brief | explanatory and controllable |
| Full reference | absent | separate PDF | full table | searchable + deep-linkable + downloads |
| Ownership | absent | generic | warranty/support | service, parts, lifecycle, limits |
| Credibility | claims | reviews | exact facts | exact facts + methods + source documents |
| Authorship | anonymous | brand voice | named creators | commentary tied to decisions |

A page can be gorgeous and score poorly. A dense catalogue can score highly while still needing visual craft. The ideal gets both.

## 19. Reference set: what to steal, what to resist

**Steal**

## 20. Experiments Tact should build

### Experiment A — beauty parity

Build two visually excellent pages from the same assets:

- story-first order;
- information-first order.

Control typography, photography, art direction, and motion quality.

Measure:

- perceived beauty;
- trust;
- purchase confidence;
- five-question retrieval time.

This tests whether facts actually reduce desire, or whether weak visual design has simply trained teams to associate specification density with ugliness.

### Experiment B — return visit

Have participants revisit after 24 hours with a new question.

Measure:

- time to a known section;
- use of anchors;
- use of browser find;
- cross-page switching;
- memory of where specs live.

A product page is often a reference document after the first visit. Design should improve with familiarity.

### Experiment C — combined page versus brochure + specs split

Compare:

1. one product page with sticky Overview / Compare / Specs / Docs anchors;
2. a cinematic brochure page plus a separate technical-spec page.

Tasks should require bouncing between emotional and technical questions.

Measure context switches, lost position, duplicate reading, and time.

### Experiment D — dimensions as hero art

Create a hero where the dimension blueprint occupies as much visual importance as the glamour photograph.

Measure perceived beauty, technical credibility, and fit-decision accuracy.

This directly tests the idea that a blueprint can be seductive.

### Experiment E — “differences only” comparison

Give three variants with 30 shared specs and 8 meaningful differences.

Compare a full matrix with a differences-only default.

Measure decision time and missed constraints. Then reveal full rows on request.

### Experiment F — evidence captions

Use the same gallery twice:

- poetic captions;
- evidence captions describing material, scale, mechanism, configuration, or test condition.

Measure recall and confidence.

### Experiment G — the external-search tax

Instrument a page and allow normal web browsing.

Count every external search needed to verify:

- dimensions;
- compatibility;
- price;
- test methodology;
- service;
- warranty;
- variant differences.

The target page should make external research optional for qualification.

## 21. Principles worth carrying into Tact

1. **Decision first.** Put facts that can eliminate the product before facts that merely decorate it.
2. **Beauty can carry data.** Drawings, tables, numbers, captions, and diagrams can be the art direction.
3. **Story earns space through consequence.** Explain why, how, measured result, and compromise.
4. **A photograph should have a job.** Appearance, scale, material, use, service, or evidence.
5. **Motion should reveal time.** Mechanisms and state changes deserve animation; ceremony gets a small budget.
6. **Comparison is a core interaction.** Normalize rows and expose compromises.
7. **Blueprints beat prose for geometry.** Show dimensions, interfaces, clearances, and relations.
8. **Progressive disclosure keeps depth close.** Key facts first, full reference one action away.
9. **Credits increase credibility.** Name the people and disciplines behind serious work.
10. **Publish the boring edges.** Warranty, limits, parts, production life, test conditions, docs, compatibility, and support periods create trust.
11. **Design for the tenth visit.** A product page becomes a reference surface once desire has been established.
12. **Let the object do the seducing.** If the engineering, proportions, materials, interaction, and results are beautiful, show them directly.

The ideal product page has the confidence of a museum catalogue and the usefulness of a datasheet. It lets a person fall in love with the object while learning exactly what they would be buying.
